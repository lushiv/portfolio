Executive summary

During my 2019 internship at Ibriz AI, I worked as an Intern Researcher & Analyst on a brainwave data-acquisition project. The fieldwork involved daily visits with meditating monks, fitting a wireless EEG headset, monitoring signal quality, recording sessions, and preparing raw datasets for data scientists in Silicon Valley.

The goal described in the project brief was to create structured EEG data that could support later analysis of brain activity during meditation. My contribution centered on the field collection workflow and the quality and organization of its data, not on claiming a completed predictive model or clinical finding.

Key terms

EEG
Electroencephalography, a non-invasive recording of electrical activity detected by sensors placed on the scalp.
Bio-signal
An electrical signal produced by a living body, including brain activity, heartbeats, and muscle movement.
Electrode
A sensor contact on the headset that picks up very small electrical signals from the scalp.
Impedance
The electrical resistance at the electrode and skin contact. Lower impedance generally supports a clearer recording.
Raw data
Sensor values recorded before downstream filtering, feature extraction, or modeling.
BCI
A brain-computer interface, a system for acquiring and interpreting signals associated with brain activity.

The challenge and research objective

The brief frames the challenge as observing meditation outside a tightly controlled laboratory, where sessions may not reflect experienced monks' usual practice. Field visits also introduce electrical noise, movement, and practical constraints around fitting and comfort. The project objective was to:

  1. Collect EEG recordings during meditation in the participants' field setting.
  2. Check signal quality and maintain a repeatable recording process.
  3. Organize session context and prepare standardized data for further analysis.

Equipment and software

  • Headset: Emotiv EPOC+ 14-channel wireless EEG device.
  • Sensors: 14 saline-soaked felt-pad electrodes plus two reference sensors for scalp coverage.
  • Connectivity: Bluetooth 2.4 GHz or the proprietary USB dongle for real-time streaming.
  • Software: Emotiv Xavier and EmotivPRO desktop tools for monitoring and raw data logging.
  • Formats: The brief identifies CSV and EDF for raw recordings, with CSV and JSON described for structured session metadata.

Fieldwork workflow

The daily process combined device preparation, participant comfort, calibration, continuous recording, and careful data organization:

  1. Hydrate the headset pads: Prepare saline solution and moisten the felt pads to establish electrical contact between each electrode and the scalp.
  2. Fit and calibrate: Position the headset using the international 10-20 placement approach, then adjust the sensor arms for stable contact without unnecessary pressure.
  3. Check impedance: Confirm the software shows green impedance indicators across all 14 electrode nodes before recording.
  4. Record a baseline: Capture eyes-open and eyes-closed readings for roughly two to three minutes.
  5. Record the meditation session: Stream the EEG and log timestamps when a change in meditation stage was observed or reported.
  6. Review signal artifacts: Watch for environmental interference and noise associated with muscle movement, jaw clenching, or eye blinks.
  7. Prepare the handoff: Organize session metadata such as age, years of meditation practice, session length, and environment, then export files for downstream analysis.

Frequency bands in the project brief

The source brief describes five EEG frequency bands and their common interpretive context. These associations are background framing, not results validated by this project.

BandRangeContext described in the brief
Delta0.5 to 4 HzThe brief associates this band with deep sleep and restorative states, and describes it as elevated in deeper states.
Theta4 to 8 HzThe brief discusses this band in relation to deep relaxation and meditative concentration.
Alpha8 to 12 HzThe brief associates this band with calmness and mental clarity, particularly during early to mid meditation.
Beta12 to 30 HzThe brief associates this band with active thought and expects it to decrease as meditation deepens.
Gamma30 to 100+ HzThe brief reports higher activity during compassionate focus; no raw analysis or statistical validation is included.

The source brief does not provide raw recordings, sample size, statistical tests, or independent validation alongside these interpretations. They should not be treated as study findings, diagnostic measurements, or medical guidance.

Data handoff and proposed downstream use

The brief describes organizing session metadata, converting recordings into structured formats, and securely transferring datasets to data-science collaborators in Silicon Valley. It lists several intended directions for later work:

  • Training classifiers to explore whether raw EEG inputs could identify deep meditation states.
  • Developing neurofeedback tools intended to guide non-meditators toward calmer states.
  • Using experienced meditators' recordings as a proposed low-stress, high-focus comparison for studying workplace stress.

These are proposed applications in the project brief. This case study does not claim that a classifier, neurofeedback product, or mental-health measure was completed or validated.

Challenges and learning

Challenges faced

  • Field variability: Manage electrical interference from power lines, fans, and nearby electronics outside a controlled lab.
  • Sensor drying: Re-hydrate saline pads during extended sessions while minimizing disruption.
  • Participant comfort: Keep the headset tolerable during prolonged stillness, including for elderly monks.

What I learned

  • Real-time bio-signal monitoring, artifact awareness, and electrode calibration.
  • How consistent field protocols connect local acquisition work to an international data pipeline.
  • Why cognitive and neuroscience interpretations require careful measurement and validation.

Conclusion

The internship placed my work between field research and data science: preparing EEG equipment, supporting meditation-session recording, checking signal quality, and organizing datasets for the next stage of analysis. It was an early lesson in how careful acquisition and clear limits on interpretation matter when human bio-signals meet AI research.

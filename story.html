Personal story / Darchula, Nepal

From taking things apart to building systems.

Radios, repair benches, and one early internet connection taught me to keep asking: how does this work?

I was born on 18 May 1998 in Darchula, a remote hill district of Nepal. As a child, I was fascinated by black-and-white televisions, radios, cassette players, and FM sets. Watching people repair electrical devices in the village made me wonder what was inside them—and how a broken thing could work again.

Around 2011, my cousin showed me Facebook on a Nokia C201 using UC Browser and helped me create my first account. I started exploring search engines and web pages, carrying the same question from the repair bench into the digital world: how does this work?

After my SLC, I moved to Kathmandu to study Electrical and Electronics Engineering. I worked in mobile repair, computer and laptop sales, and service centres while studying, then explored web research, scraping, AI, blockchain, automation, and IoT. That curiosity grew into backend, blockchain, and fintech development. I completed my BCA while working, and have kept building from there.

Today, I work on backend and distributed systems, blockchain services, and AI integrations, while continuing to explore IoT, sensors, and robotics. It has been a gradual journey from learning how to repair devices to building systems people rely on.

What stayed with me

Stay curious. Learn by taking things apart, asking questions, and doing the work.

Keep building. Turn what you learn into something useful to someone else.

The path so far

From repair benches to backend systems

Every step added a new layer: understanding devices, helping people fix them, studying how the web works, then building the services behind it.

Childhood

Curiosity starts with the hardware

Black-and-white television, radios, cassette players, and FM sets caught my attention. Watching electrical repairs in the village made me curious about how devices worked—and how to bring them back to life.

2011

My first window onto the internet

My cousin showed me Facebook on a Nokia C201 through UC Browser and helped me create my first account. I started learning to use search engines and explore web pages—a new kind of curiosity joined the old one.

2013–2016

Studying electronics, learning through repair

After SLC, I moved to Kathmandu for a three-year diploma in Electrical and Electronics Engineering. Alongside my studies, I worked with mobile phones, laptops, and computers—repairing hardware, troubleshooting software, and helping customers at shops and service centres.

2017–2018

Researching what the web could do

I explored web research and scraping, AI, blockchain, automation, and IoT, including work with an IT company. I was learning how these technologies connected and where they could be useful.

2018–present

Making time for community work

Alongside my technology work, I’ve stayed involved with Help For Happiness as a volunteer, coordinating directly with its founder and contributing to IT counselling events and awareness activities on cybersecurity and data policy.

2019–2020

Turning research into backend and fintech work

I moved into backend, blockchain, and fintech development, building on years of hands-on troubleshooting and independent research. Alongside work, I completed my BCA.

Since 2020

Building systems and continuing to explore

I now work across backend architecture, distributed services, blockchain, and AI integrations. My research continues toward IoT, sensors, and robotics—still guided by the same question that began at the repair bench.

Community / Since 2018

Technology matters most when it helps people.

Volunteering alongside my technology work

A steady connection to community work

Since 2018, I’ve been involved with Help For Happiness, coordinating directly with its founder and volunteering at IT counselling events and awareness activities. This has included helping people think about cybersecurity and data policy in everyday digital life.

It’s a different kind of problem-solving: listening first, making technical ideas understandable, and helping people make informed choices.

Not every project worked. Each one taught me something.

Before my work became more focused on software engineering, I tried building small online projects of my own. Some did not last; one opened a door I never expected.

Blockchain media / Nepal

Blockmandu: a project that reached beyond the website

I built Blockmandu around blockchain news and coverage. In 2019, we published Korea Blockchain Week articles featuring Gavin Wood and Brittany Kaiser, and shared event promotion through the site and social media.
